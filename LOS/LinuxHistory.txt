Linux was created in 1991 by Linus Torvalds, a Finnish computer science student. Inspired by the UNIX operating system, he wanted a free and open-source alternative that anyone could use, modify, and share. He combined his Linux kernel with tools from the GNU Project, founded by Richard Stallman in 1983 to promote free software.

The GNU/Linux system is built on the philosophy of freedom, collaboration, and transparency. Users have the freedom to view, change, and distribute the source code. This open model has led to rapid innovation, global community support, and wide adoption across servers, desktops, mobile devices, and embedded systems.
